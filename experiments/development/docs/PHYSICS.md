# Physical families and numerical reference

This experiment uses six controlled, dimensionless, periodic one-dimensional
field equations. They cover wave propagation, nonlinear transport, unstable
pattern dynamics, linear transport with diffusion, bistable phase dynamics, and
coupled chemical reactions with diffusion. Allen–Cahn and Gray–Scott are distinct
equation families within the broader reaction-diffusion class; six equations
should not be described as six statistically independent kinds of physics.

The reference is a declared finite-dimensional grid system, integrated in
float64. Time refinement verifies its temporal accuracy. It does not prove
convergence to a continuum PDE, physical fidelity of a device, or sim-to-real
transfer. There are no measurements from an actual reactor in this experiment.

## Common definitions

The grid is `x_j = j L/N`, `j=0,...,N-1`, with no duplicated endpoint. The standard
configuration has `N=32`. Let `theta=2*pi*x/L`. A scalar action `a_t` is held
constant during each observation interval. The action values are generated by
linear interpolation between independent uniform `[-1,1]` knots eight
observation intervals apart. Thus the recorded action varies smoothly across
observations while the simulator uses a zero-order hold inside each interval.

Each family has three coefficients. Each coefficient independently ranges over
`[0.85,1.15]` times its center for training and in-distribution data. The declared
coefficient-OOD split puts every coordinate independently in `[1.30,1.50]` times
its center. This is a specific joint extrapolation, not comprehensive OOD testing.
The misspecified prior multiplies the actual coefficient vector once by
`[0.70,1.30,1.40]`. These altered coefficients are an imperfect supplied simulator;
the true coefficient values must not become extra selector features.

for compatibility. They are not additional families in this six-family design.

## Equations

### Damped driven wave

`u_t = v`

`v_t = c^2 u_xx - gamma v - kappa u + 0.5 a_t sin(theta)`

The unforced quadratic energy dissipates under positive damping. Initial
displacement and velocity contain independently phased modes 1–3; the velocity
amplitudes are half the displacement amplitudes. Fourier differentiation and
RK4 advance the coupled first-order system. Fine/check use 4/8 substeps; the
substep count grows for grids larger than 32 to keep the declared linear
coefficient range inside RK4's stability region.

### Viscous Burgers

`u_t = nu u_xx - alpha u u_x - delta u + 0.4 a_t sin(theta)`

The implementation evaluates the quadratic transport as
`-(alpha/2) D_x P[(P u)^2]`, where `P` retains Fourier indices strictly below
`N/3`. It is this truncated finite-dimensional equation that defines the target.
Initial modes 1–3 have random phases, with an additional random mean.

### Kuramoto–Sivashinsky

`u_t = -alpha u_xx - beta u_xxxx - gamma u u_x`
`      + a_t [0.1 sin(theta) + 0.05 cos(2 theta)]`

Negative diffusion destabilizes long wavelengths; hyperdiffusion damps short
wavelengths. The quadratic transport uses the same declared truncation as
Burgers. The initial field has modes 1–5. This equation is capable of complex
unstable dynamics; that fact does not imply every short sampled trajectory is
chaotic. The time-refinement audit also starts from a state evolved for 128
intervals. A minimum grid of 32 preserves dissipative modes after truncation.

### Advection–diffusion

`u_t = D u_xx - v u_x - gamma u`
`      + a_t [0.4 sin(theta) + 0.15 cos(3 theta)]`

Initial modes 1–3 have independent phases. Each Fourier coefficient has an
exact affine update while the action is held fixed, using `exp(dt*lambda)` and
`dt*phi_1(dt*lambda)`. The first derivative of the real Nyquist cosine is zero
on an even collocation grid; its second derivative still produces diffusion.
This special case is tested explicitly. Coarse, fine, and check consequently
coincide up to roundoff, so an analytic Fourier-mode test is more informative
than time refinement alone. This linear system provides a controlled contrast
to nonlinear transport.

### Forced Allen–Cahn

`u_t = D u_xx + r (u - u^3) + b a_t sin(theta)`

The signed order parameter initially contains modes 1–3 with random phases and
moderate amplitude. The double-well reaction promotes states near `-1` and `+1`;
diffusion smooths interfaces. Without forcing, the discrete free energy is
`mean[-D u laplacian(u)/2 + r (u^2-1)^2/4]`. The numerical audit checks that this
energy decreases over its unforced probe. Forcing may increase the energy.
Allen–Cahn gradient-flow structure and numerical energy/maximum-bound issues
are discussed by [Ju, Li and Qiao (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04532).

The linear part is `D laplacian + r`; the cubic term is evaluated on the full
collocation grid. These cubic products are not dealiased. This is intentional:
the fixed-grid collocation ODE is the synthetic system being tested.

### Controlled Gray–Scott

`U_t = D U_xx - U V^2 + F_t(x) (1-U)`

`V_t = (D/2) V_xx + U V^2 - [F_t(x)+k] V`

`F_t(x) = F [1 + 0.35 a_t sin(theta)]`

The two concentration fields start from smooth, independently shifted
one- and two-lobed finite-amplitude seeds. Concentrations are positive at
initialization. The feed is always positive for the declared action range and
keeps driving an open chemical system. This controlled 1D variant follows the
two-species Gray–Scott reaction structure; it does not reproduce Pearson's 2D
pattern experiments. See [Pearson (1993)](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9304003).

Diffusion is the linear part; all cubic reactions and the spatial feed are
evaluated in physical grid space without dealiasing. No positivity projection,
state clipping, or concentration floor is used. Positivity is checked on the
sampled reference trajectories, not asserted as an unconditional property of
ETDRK4. The isolated reaction conserves `U+V`; the complete open system does not.

The removal-rate center was chosen before model experiments using finite,
nontrivial ID/OOD simulator trajectories. At center 0.055 the specified joint
OOD shift made the product nearly extinct by the end of the chosen window; the
declared center 0.04 retains richer OOD transients. This numerical design choice
must not be represented as a result of a preregistered model comparison.

## Numerical integration and validation

Burgers, KS, Allen–Cahn, and Gray–Scott use the fourth-order exponential
Runge–Kutta scheme of [Cox and Matthews (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.2002.6995).
The exponential linear part handles diffusion stiffness. Stable real phi
functions use a Taylor branch near zero; coefficients are formed in float64
and then cast to the state precision. Advection–diffusion uses a corresponding
complex phi function for its exact affine map. `coarse`, `fine`, and `check`
normally use 1, 4, and 8 substeps respectively. Burgers/KS increase substeps for
grids above 64. These explicit nonlinear schemes are not unconditionally
stable for arbitrary perturbed states or arbitrary coefficients.

The numerical tests cover:

- Fine/check temporal convergence and applicable unforced energy or positivity
  checks for all six families, including evolved nonlinear states.
- Finite eight-trajectory ID/OOD references through all 184 intervals of the
  full 24-burn + 160-observation design, with late trajectory movement.
- Finite float32 state, action, and parameter gradients of the coarse prior on
  perturbed evolved states, for both correct and coefficient-misspecified priors.
- Exact translated/damped Fourier modes, Nyquist handling, the homogeneous
  Allen–Cahn analytic solution, Gray–Scott reaction stoichiometry, and the
  product-free feed solution.

The runtime audit records its tolerances and results rather than silently
assuming that a completed simulation is an accurate reference. Reference
generation raises on nonfinite states. A failed numerical audit should stop
scientific interpretation of the associated experiment. Augmentation controls
on nonlinear families must use numerical output matching; the old affine-wave
target scaling identity is not valid for Allen–Cahn, Gray–Scott, Burgers, or KS.

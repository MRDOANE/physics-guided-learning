# Study design and interpretation limits

## Prediction task

The input is a short history of fully observed physical states, a known action, and parameter information. A neural network predicts the next state. Predictions are then fed back into the network to evaluate longer rollouts. The primary forecast horizon is 64 steps; 96-step and coefficient-shift evaluations are secondary. The code defines the normalization and per-trajectory error calculation.

There are three architectures: a transformer with separate encoder blocks, a transformer with one block reused three times, and a small one-dimensional Fourier neural operator. A repeated transformer block is computation within one prediction, not another physical time step. The model is trained separately for each equation and configuration. Width and update budgets are shared; parameter counts, FLOPs, and wall time are not matched.

## Physical guidance

All augmented candidates retain supervised training on clean numerical transitions. Their additional loss uses perturbed state histories and a next-state target generated by a coarse physical operator. The unaugmented candidate has no such auxiliary loss. The augmented candidates use independent noise, spatially smooth low-frequency perturbations, or response-matched independent noise. The settings include a physical-loss weight of 0.1 and perturbation magnitude of 0.05.

Independent and smooth perturbations have paired, equal input root-mean-square size per sampled state and channel. The response-matched control rescales each sampled independent perturbation to match the scalar, normalized one-step response size produced by the physical operator for its paired smooth perturbation. Nonlinear bisection performs this matching. It does not match the response vector, covariance, Jacobian, or target error relative to true perturbed dynamics; rescaling also changes input perturbation amplitude. Perturbations at different history positions need not form a physically consistent trajectory.

An augmentation-versus-none comparison measures the combined effect of extra physical supervision and perturbation. An unperturbed auxiliary-teacher control was not included, so the comparison does not isolate the effect of noise alone.

## Physical mismatch

Physical operators can have correct coefficients, biased coefficients, or an omitted term. The coefficient perturbation is deterministic; it is not a study of noisy coefficient measurement. Per-regime standardization cancels a positive global coefficient rescaling in the neural input, while the physical operator generating auxiliary targets still changes. The physical mismatch primarily affects that auxiliary target generator.

The reference and coarse operators share a simulator family. Numerical audits check specified temporal and spatial refinements. These tests do not establish general continuum convergence or validation against laboratory observations.

## Experimental sequence

1. The development experiment completed 864 fits across six equations, two prior settings, four augmentation arms, three architectures, and six training seeds. Gray–Scott was originally held out for its first selector analysis.
2. The follow-up reused validation information from all six previously observed equations, including Gray–Scott. Those observations are development evidence for the new selector.
3. The resolution stage completed 288 fits: three equations, two grids, two prior settings, two augmentation arms, three architectures, and four seeds.
4. The new selector stage completed 432 fits: two new equations, three prior settings, four arms, three architectures, and six seeds.

The follow-up source includes a filtered 864-row development bank containing allowed validation fields, plus provenance hashes. Test errors were excluded from selector fitting. The archived selection inputs, decisions, and evaluation plans document the local freeze. It was not an external timestamped registration, and earlier results informed the follow-up design.

## Selector

Ridge regression predicts the validation log-loss ratio relative to no augmentation from diagnostics, architecture, augmentation arm, interactions, and short training probes. The guard considers both the full development fit and leave-one-development-equation-out fits. Its worst predicted ratio must favor the candidate and its probe must beat the unaugmented probe; otherwise the rule chooses no augmentation. Hyperparameter choices were made using development data.

The selector uses target training and validation data. Its guard is a heuristic, with no general performance guarantee. The full experiment trains every candidate to observe outcomes. Target-route costs are reconstructed from measured components and are not actual savings realized while building the research bank.

## Statistical scope

The confirmation analysis uses geometric aggregation of per-stratum MSE ratios, paired training seeds, and complete test trajectories. Its intervals are conditional on the generated training datasets and the fixed tested equations and architectures. Resolution tests and selector tests use separate Holm correction families. Displayed 95% intervals are pointwise; the Holm p-values control the specified test families.

A supported positive or negative effect does not establish a universal law. Pooled effects can hide architecture differences. For example, the correct-prior transport result favors smooth augmentation in the pooled analysis, while the transformer and looped-transformer point estimates worsen and the FNO improves. Secondary coefficient-shift results also expose selector limitations. See [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

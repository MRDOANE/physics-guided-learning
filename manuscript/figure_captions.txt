Figure 1. Absolute forecast error with incorrect coefficients and missing equation terms. Each point is mean normalized squared error across 64 ordinary test trajectories, averaged over a 64-step recursive forecast. The selected neural result averages the candidate chosen by 16-step validation error for each archived training seed. All selected neural candidates used the Fourier neural operator. Error bars show exploratory pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals; calibrated physical fits and the training dataset are held fixed. Panel (a) shows one setting per physical family using the highest available grid; panel (b) shows both missing-term settings. The number beside each family is the number of grid sites. The horizontal axis is logarithmic. Errors near numerical precision reflect noiseless data generated from the available equation family with a coefficient distortion represented by the calibration model.

Figure 2. Architecture-specific effects of smooth augmentation at 64 grid points. Each cell gives 100 × (1 − R), where R is the geometric mean of the paired smooth-to-no-augmentation mean squared error ratios across four training seeds. The error calculation uses 64-step test rollouts. Positive values indicate reduced error after adding the physics-based training examples. Negative values indicate increased error; for example, −512.9% corresponds to approximately 6.13 times the baseline error. These are descriptive point estimates. The linear color scale is symmetric around zero and includes the full range of observed values.

Figure 3. Matched CPU execution times across 30 physical settings. Each point is a setting’s median time across five blocks of 20 forward calls. Circles and squares indicate 32 and 64 grid sites. Boxes show the median and interquartile range across settings, with conventional 1.5-interquartile-range whiskers. Calls used one trajectory and one CPU intra-operation thread with inputs resident in memory. Physics timing includes the coarse numerical step and application of fitted coefficient multipliers. Neural timing includes normalization and denormalization and uses initialized copies of the archived architectures; trained checkpoints were unavailable. These execution measurements are separate from the saved trained-model accuracy measurements. The test excludes data transfer, history updates, error calculation, fitting and full-rollout overhead. All measurements used PyTorch 2.8 on the same CPU environment.

Figure S1. Perturbation comparisons with correct physical coefficients. Smooth augmentation is compared with spatially independent noise (a) and with noise rescaled to match the one-step change produced by the physical model (b). Smooth and independent perturbations in panel a have matched input root-mean-square magnitude. Each point is the geometric mean of paired 64-step mean squared error ratios across three architectures and six training seeds at 32 grid points. Values below one favor smooth augmentation. Intervals use 50,000 crossed bootstrap resamples of paired seeds and complete test trajectories. The intervals retain the original Bonferroni adjustment across all 24 development contrasts, giving a 99.792% interval for each plotted comparison. The panel displays 12 of those contrasts.

Figure S2. Repetition of selected augmentation effects at two spatial resolutions. The ratio compares smooth augmentation with training without the auxiliary physics loss. Separate models were trained at 32 and 64 grid points, using four seeds at each resolution. Points pool the transformer, looped transformer, and Fourier neural operator through the geometric mean of paired 64-step mean squared error ratios. Error bars are pointwise 95% intervals from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples of seeds and complete test trajectories. Values below one indicate lower error with smooth augmentation; values above one indicate higher error. The six tests at 64 grid points use a separate Holm correction reported in the text. The intervals shown here are pointwise.

Figure 1. Perturbation comparisons with correct physical coefficients. Smooth augmentation is compared with spatially independent noise (a) and with noise rescaled to match the one-step change produced by the physical model (b). Smooth and independent perturbations in panel a have matched input root-mean-square magnitude. Each point is the geometric mean of paired 64-step mean squared error ratios across three architectures and six training seeds at 32 grid points. Values below one favor smooth augmentation. Intervals use 50,000 crossed bootstrap resamples of paired seeds and complete test trajectories. The intervals retain the original Bonferroni adjustment across all 24 development contrasts, giving a 99.792% interval for each plotted comparison. The panel displays 12 of those contrasts.

Figure 2. Repetition of selected augmentation effects at two spatial resolutions. The ratio compares smooth augmentation with training without the auxiliary physics loss. Separate models were trained at 32 and 64 grid points, using four seeds at each resolution. Points pool the transformer, looped transformer, and Fourier neural operator through the geometric mean of paired 64-step mean squared error ratios. Error bars are pointwise 95% intervals from 20,000 paired bootstrap resamples of seeds and complete test trajectories. Values below one indicate lower error with smooth augmentation; values above one indicate higher error. The six tests at 64 grid points use a separate Holm correction reported in the text. The intervals shown here are pointwise.

Figure 3. Architecture-specific effects of smooth augmentation at 64 grid points. Each cell gives 100 × (1 − R), where R is the geometric mean of the paired smooth-to-no-augmentation mean squared error ratios across four training seeds. The error calculation uses 64-step test rollouts. Positive values indicate reduced error after adding the physics-based training examples. Negative values indicate increased error; for example, −512.9% corresponds to approximately 6.13 times the baseline error. These are descriptive point estimates. The linear color scale is symmetric around zero and includes the full range of observed values.
